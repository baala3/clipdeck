CLIPDECK

Everything you copied, one hotkey away.

Clipdeck is a small clipboard manager for Windows, macOS and Linux. It remembers what you copy and pastes it back from a menu right at your cursor.

Copy as usual. Find it again later.

Clipdeck sits quietly in your tray or menu bar and keeps a History of what you copy, so the thing you copied ten minutes ago is never lost.

  • 01

    A History of your copies

    Text and images are saved the moment you copy them. Keep the last 200 Clips, or as many as 2000.

  • 02

    A menu at your cursor

    One hotkey opens a native menu where your mouse already is. Pick a Clip and it is pasted straight into the app you are working in.

  • 03

    Pin what you reuse

    Keep an address, a reply or a command in the Pinned list, where it stays until you remove it. You can write your own, too.

  • 04

    Fast from the keyboard

    Number keys pick the first ten Clips. On Windows, right-click a Clip to edit, pin or delete it; anywhere, hold a modifier while you click. Every hotkey can be changed.

  • 05

    Private by design

    Your Clips stay on your computer. Copies from password managers are skipped, and you can exclude any app or pause capture.

  • 06

    Looks after itself

    Clipdeck starts when you log in and checks for updates once a day. It always asks before installing one.

Screenshots and pictures, not just text

Copy an image or take a screenshot and it lands in History beside your text. Choose it and it is pasted back as an image.

  • Each image shows a thumbnail and its size, so you can tell one screenshot from the next.
  • On Windows, rest on an image to see it larger before you paste.
  • Pin an image to keep it, just like text.

Three steps, no new habits.

Nothing to learn and nothing to organise. Clipdeck stays out of the way until you ask for it.

  1. Step 01

    Copy like you always do

    Clipdeck notices each copy and adds it to your History. Copying the same thing twice just moves it back to the top.

    Your usual copyCtrlC
  2. Step 02

    Press Ctrl Shift D

    The menu opens at your cursor with History and Pinned together. Long Clips are shortened to one line, and older ones are tucked into folders.

    Opens the menuCtrlShiftD
  3. Step 03

    Choose a Clip

    Click it or press its number. Clipdeck pastes it where you were typing and gets out of the way.

One line, and it is running.

Paste the command for your computer. It downloads the latest version, installs it for your user only and starts Clipdeck.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit

Your computer

Open PowerShell and run:

irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/baala3/clipdeck/main/install.ps1 | iex

No administrator rights needed. Clipdeck then lives in the notification area, next to the clock.

macOS

macOS 11 or later, Apple silicon and Intel

Your computer

Open Terminal and run:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/baala3/clipdeck/main/install.sh | sh

Clipdeck then lives in the menu bar. The first time you choose a Clip, macOS asks you to allow Accessibility so it can paste for you.

Linux

64-bit Intel or AMD, in an X11 session (not Wayland yet)

Your computer

Open a terminal and run:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/baala3/clipdeck/main/install.sh | sh

No administrator rights needed. Clipdeck installs as a single AppImage and then lives in the system tray.

Install Clipdeck
Is Clipdeck really free?

Yes. There is no subscription, no paid tier and no ads. The code is open source under the MIT License.

Why a command and not a download button?

Clipdeck is not signed with a paid developer certificate. Windows warns about an unsigned app downloaded with a browser, and macOS refuses to open one. The one-line command installs the same app without either problem.

The scripts are short and you can read them first: install.ps1 for Windows and install.sh for macOS and Linux. Some Windows 11 PCs have Smart App Control switched on, which blocks unsigned apps either way; the install guide explains.

Where are my Clips stored?

On your own computer, in your user folder: %LOCALAPPDATA%\dev.clipdeck.app on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/dev.clipdeck.app on macOS and ~/.local/share/dev.clipdeck.app on Linux. There is no account and no cloud sync.

Does it save my passwords?

Password managers mark what they copy as not meant for clipboard tools, and Clipdeck respects that. You can also add any app to the Excluded apps list in Settings, or pause capture from the menu whenever you like.

Why does macOS ask for the Accessibility permission?

Pasting into another app is something macOS only allows with that permission. Until you grant it, choosing a Clip still copies it and you paste with ⌘ V yourself.

How do I uninstall it?

On Windows, use "Add or remove programs". On macOS, quit Clipdeck from its menu bar icon and drag it from Applications to the Trash. On Linux, quit it from its menu and delete ~/.local/bin/Clipdeck.AppImage. The install guide lists the leftover files you can delete.

Does it work on Wayland?

Not yet. On Linux, Clipdeck needs an X11 session, which most distributions still offer on the login screen, often as "Xorg". Wayland keeps apps from watching the clipboard, setting hotkeys and pasting for you in the ways Clipdeck does all three.
